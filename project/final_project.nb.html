campus_crime <- read_csv(
  "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dwillis/jour405/refs/heads/main/data/usm_oncampus_crime_2015_2024.csv",
  col_types = cols(
    campus_id = col_character(),
    ope_id = col_character(),
    zip = col_character(),
    us_scope = col_character(),
    older_than_latest_collection = col_character()

  show_col_types = FALSE

USMCrime <- campus_crime |>
  filter(institution_name %in% usm_schools) |>
  mutate(is_primary_campus = campus_id %in% primary_campus_ids) |>
  arrange(institution_name, campus_id, crime_year)

USMCrime_primary <- USMCrime |>
  filter(is_primary_campus)

In the block above, I filtered the USM schools (excluding University of Baltimore, UMGC and the University of Maryland Center for Environmental Science) down to their main campuses. This was just a preliminary block to get all the data sorted.

USMCrime <- read_csv("Criminal_Offenses_On_campus.csv")

The data comes from the U.S. Department of Education’s Campus Safety and Security Data Analysis Cutting Tool, which allows users to download campus crime and fire data. For this project, I downloaded data from 2015-2024 from all University System of Maryland schools, excluding University of Baltimore (primarily graduate and professional students), University of Maryland, Global Campus (mainly online and does not have a main campus) and Center for Environmental Science (a research center).

The Department of Education pulls this data from the OPE Campus Safety and Security Statistics website database, where the USM reports their crime and fire statistics annually.

I collected the data using the ‘Download Custom Data’ tool. I selected the schools I will analyze, made the time frame 2015-2024 (2025 data is not available online yet), chose ‘Con-campus’ for the location and narrowed the crime category down to ‘Criminal Offenses - On campus.’ The data contains the school names, institution sizes, the survey years and crime categories. The crime categories are Murder/Non-negligent manslaughter, Negligent manslaughter, Sex offenses - Forcible, Rape, Fondling, Sex Offenses - Non-forcible, Incest, Statutory rape, Robbery, Aggravated assault, Burglary, Motor vehicle theft and Arson.

The columns most important to my analysis are Murder, both Sex offenses, Robbery and Aggravated assault. These will help me analyze violent crimes across multiple situations and environments. The incident counts for these are there, but I will have to turn these into rates– should I calculate the rates by the institution size provided in the data to get incidents per 1,000 students? Since I’m looking at on-campus incidents, I may need help finding the on-campus student numbers if I run into trouble with that.
